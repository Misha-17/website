01 / FROM REPRESENTATION TO CONTROLRESEARCH MANUSCRIPT

A readable signal is not a control knob.

Persona information was detectable inside the models. Injecting that information back into their activations did not reliably produce the intended behaviour.

SCALE: 0–5 · HIGHER = STRONGER PERSONA EXPRESSION

Activation vs. baseline: p = 0.21. Adding the activation intervention to prompting: p = 0.43. Detectability and useful control came apart in this setup.

What this leaves open: the finding concerns the tested additive residual-stream interventions. It does not rule out other ways to steer a model.

02 / FROM PERSONALIZATION TO EXPERIENCEPUBLISHED THESIS

Different output doesn’t always feel more personal.

Giving an assistant personality information about a user can change its responses. Whether that helps the person feel understood is a separate question.

FROM THE THESIS · CONTROLLED USER STUDY
200

participants · 97 baseline, 103 personalized

No improvement detected in the measured felt-understanding outcomes.

In this experiment, Big Five prompt personalization did not improve the measured outcomes relative to the baseline. All reported condition effects were small (d < 0.25).

What this leaves open: this result concerns one form of personalization and one study setting. It does not establish that all personalization is ineffective.

03 / FROM INTERNAL SIGNAL TO ANSWERPUBLISHED THESIS

A better signal can stay inside the model.

On causal questions that contradict common expectations, an internal probe was more accurate than the model’s answers. That gap makes evaluation more interesting than a single final score.

798 ANTICOMMONSENSE CLADDER ITEMS · QWEN2.5-7B
70.4%

Internal probe accuracy

Measured with cross-validation.

55.5%

Output accuracy

The model’s generated answers.

A 14.9 percentage-point gap between the two measurements.

What this leaves open: probe accuracy is evidence of recoverable information, not proof of human-like causal understanding or a working intervention.
